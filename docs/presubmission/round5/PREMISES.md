## Found beyond the 40

**B1. RWM v1 has no sample-efficiency table.** The 6M-against-250M "Table 1: Comparison with model-free method"
first appears in **v2** (§5 Limitations); v1's Limitations section (`2501.10100v1.txt`, "V Limitations") is
prose only, and "250" does not occur in v1's text or HTML. `results/original_paper_figures.json`
(`sample_efficiency.where_v1` = "2501.10100v1 Table I (Limitations)"), `PAPER.template.md:1243` ("§IV-E") and
`:1779` ("Table I") place it in v1. Crosswalk id `rwm_sample_efficiency`: `new`. Owner: G12 or G15 (App. D),
via the artifact's generator. Logged in `OUT_OF_SCOPE.md`.

**B2. The imagination path uses one member, not the ensemble mean.** `robotic_world_model_lite/scripts/envs/base.py:54,68`
draw `system_dynamics_model_ids` (one random member per environment, redrawn at reset) and `:142` passes them to
`system_dynamics.forward`; `system_dynamics.py:116-121` then returns *that member's* mean (`torch.gather`), and
the history fed back is that member's prediction. With `model_ids=None` (`model_training.py:133`,
`mbpo_ppo.py:291`, our open-loop diagnostic) `forward` returns the ensemble mean (`:112-115`). The paper cites
only the second (`PAPER.template.md:689`, "feeds the ensemble **mean** back into it (`system_dynamics.py:115`)")
and never mentions `model_ids`. In both paths the epistemic term is the spread of the heads' means at that one
shared state history. So the paper's §6.4 sentence is true of the evaluation rollout, which is what we measured,
and not of the released policy-training rollout, which is closer to a per-environment single-model rollout. **X9
(G2, G3) must say which of the two it contrasts with a per-member rollout.**
