STEP 0 ANSWER
==============================================================================
  10 of the 11 persistent change points sit at offset
  [482, 503, 504, 505, 506, 507, 510, 511, 512, 514] within their episode -- i.e. one command change at the
  midpoint of every one of the ten episodes, at ~step 505 (~10.1 s).
  The remaining 1 ([7149]) is a borderline call: a short, high-variance
  excursion of the kind the push events produce, not a clean plateau step.

  => The data contains 21 commanded-velocity regime segments,
     20 of them the clean two-per-episode structure (one change at each episode midpoint)
     plus 1 extra in episode [7] -- the borderline excursion noted above.
     Regimes per episode: [2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 3, 2, 2]. Each held ~500 steps (10 s); episodes 1000 steps (20 s).
  => The ten episodes are NOT ten repetitions of one command. Every regime
     is a different (v_x, v_y) target; at a 0.10 m/s tolerance all 21 are
     distinct, spanning roughly [-0.95, +0.90] x [-0.97, +0.87] m/s in the plane.
  => A held-out episode is therefore NOT a near-duplicate of a training
     episode. It contains two velocity commands the model has not seen at
     those exact values. The split measures generalisation across commands,
     not memorisation of a single behaviour.

  Caveat: the gait itself (a ~1.85 Hz trot) is the same throughout, and all
  commands are drawn from one bounded box, so this is generalisation across
  velocity commands within one gait -- not across gaits or terrain.
