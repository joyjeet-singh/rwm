DECOMPOSING THE A/B GAP
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Two confounders have to be separated before the gap means anything.

  (i) The reference checkpoint was trained by the authors on this entire
      CSV. Protocol A's 'held-out' episodes were in its training set, so
      for THIS checkpoint A is not a generalisation measure -- it only
      becomes one for a model we train ourselves in Step 6. The A/B gap
      here therefore cannot be read as 'how much leakage flatters the
      reference'; that reading only applies from Step 6 onward.
  (ii) Protocol B lets trajectories straddle an episode reset. A reset is
      a physical discontinuity no model can predict, so the expectation is
      that crossing trajectories are penalised for a jump that is not a
      modelling failure. This is TESTED below, not assumed.

  protocol A: per-trajectory e = [0.44 0.84 0.67 0.66 0.82 0.85 0.72 0.80 1.03 0.85]
  protocol B: per-trajectory e = [0.63 2.41 0.54 0.93 2.34 1.92 0.59 2.09 0.64 0.62]
    of the 10, 5 cross a reset and 5 do not
    e over the 5 crossing    : 0.9470
    e over the 5 non-crossing: 1.5986
    -> hypothesis (ii) is REFUTED: the crossing trajectories score
       BETTER, not worse. Boundary crossing is not what drives the
       A/B gap. See the per-episode difficulty table below.

  Per-episode difficulty (20 trajectories drawn inside each episode,
  identical protocol, so the only thing that varies is the episode):

    spread across episodes: 0.601 to 1.674  (mean 1.097)
    held-out pair mean 0.694 vs all-episode mean 1.097

    => The A/B gap is dominated by WHICH episodes get sampled, not by
       boundary crossing. Protocol A draws only from the held-out pair,

       reads lower than protocol B mostly for that reason.

    CONSEQUENCE FOR THE SPLIT: the brief asks for stratification by
    velocity regime, and that is what was done -- but the correlation
    above shows commanded speed does not predict difficulty, so
    stratifying by speed does not balance difficulty. The seed-0 split
    holds out two of the easiest episodes, and protocol A is optimistic
    by roughly the pair-vs-population difference above.
    The split is NOT re-picked to fix this: choosing a held-out set by
    measured error would select on the test signal. For Step 6 the sound
    options are leave-one-episode-out over all ten, or averaging over
    several seeded splits, and reporting the spread either way.

  Ten trajectories is what the reference uses, but is it enough to
